6 checkmate patterns every beginner should know

Checkmate is the goal of every game. These patterns turn up again and again in beginner games, so learn them both to deliver and to defend.

▶ Open the game

1. Fool's mate (mate in 2)

1. f3 e5 2. g4 Qh4# White opens the f- and g-pawns, leaving the diagonal to the king wide open.

Defence: in the opening, never push the f- and g-pawns together.

2. Scholar's mate (mate in 4)

1. e4 e5 2. Bc4 Nc6 3. Qh5 Nf6?? 4. Qxf7# Queen and bishop gang up on f7, which only the king defends.

Defence: answer 3. Qh5 with 3... g6 or 3... Qe7 to cover f7, then chase the queen while developing.

3. Back-rank mate

A castled king behind three unmoved pawns (king g8, pawns f7, g7, h7) has no escape when an enemy rook or queen lands on the back rank.

Defence: when you have a spare move, push the h-pawn one square (h3 or h6) to give the king air.

4. The two-rook ladder

The rooks take turns: one checks, the other stops the king stepping back. Rank by rank the king is pushed to the edge and mated there. If the king walks up to a rook, swing that rook to the far side first.

5. King and queen against a lone king

  1. Box the enemy king in with your queen, a knight's move away from it.
  2. Shrink the box step by step until the king is on the edge.
  3. Bring your own king up and deliver mate with the queen on the edge.

Watch for stalemate: before each move, check the enemy king still has a legal move. No moves and not in check is an instant draw.

6. Smothered mate

The king is boxed in by its own pieces, for example king h8, rook g8, pawns g7 and h7. A white knight jumps in with Nf7#. Only a knight can jump over pieces, so only a knight mates like this.

Practise in the game

The daily puzzle and the Road to master levels include mating puzzles. Then play Easy and set yourself the goal of finishing with pattern 4 or 5.

FAQ

What is the fastest checkmate?

Fool's mate in two: 1. f3 e5 2. g4 Qh4#, after White opens the f- and g-pawns.

How do I stop Scholar's mate?

When the white queen comes to h5, play g6 or Qe7 to cover f7.

What is a back-rank mate?

A king boxed in by its own pawns is checkmated by a rook or queen on the back rank. Prevent it by pushing the h-pawn one square.
